**Reading the Box Plot Anatomy**
A box plot splits your data into quarters based on mathematical percentiles:

The Main Box: Contains the middle 50% of your data (the bulk of normal apartments in Gurgaon, mostly sitting between 1,500 and 3,500 sqft).

The Orange Line: The median (the absolute middle property size).

The Upper Whisker (The horizontal line around 6,000): This represents the statistical boundary for a "normal maximum" property size based on the distribution variance.
[This is what happened when i tried applying these methods to some Individual values which exceeded even 33000 sqft.]

The Individual Circles: Every single circle floating above that 6,000 whisker is a mathematical outlier.

**The Strategy: The Interquartile Range (IQR) Formula**

Instead of guessing a arbitrary cutoff number like "let's just delete everything over 6,000 sqft",
professionals use a mathematical framework called the IQR Method to automatically calculate the exact threshold where a data point becomes an outlier.

The formula works by calculating the distance between the 25th percentile (Q_1) and the 75th percentile (Q_3):IQR = Q_3 - Q_1

The mathematical upper boundary line is defined as:
Upper Fence = Q_3 + (1.5 * IQR)

Any property with a total_area larger than this Upper Fence gets deleted from our training data.

+++++++   4
***Checking for Multi-Collinearity (The Senior Check)***
We had to remove, total_area, Rate_per_sqft, Price from our dataset, 
Price = total_area * Rate_per_sqft

If you leave Rate_per_sqft inside your training features to predict Price, your machine learning model doesn't have to learn anything about bedrooms, localities, or property types.
It can just multiply total_area by Rate_per_sqft and get a perfect score.

Why is this a problem?
When your client deploys your model to production on Upwork or Fiverr, a user will input: 
"I have a 3 BHK in Sector 45 with 2,000 sqft. How much is it worth?" The user will not know the Rate_per_sqft—that's what they are hiring the model to find out! 
If your model relies on Rate_per_sqft to make its prediction, it will crash in the real world because that information won't be available.
